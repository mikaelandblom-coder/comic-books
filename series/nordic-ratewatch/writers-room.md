# Ratewatch Writers' Room: making it actually funny

The risk with character comedy is **"on-character but not funny"**: every line tells you who someone
is (*"I love war!"*, *"No cap!"*, *"Let me explain the maths"*) and none of them is a joke. A line only counts
as a joke if it **surprises** you. The character is just the lens that produces the surprise.

---

## 1. What already works (from the legacy episodes)

| Line | Why it's funny |
|---|---|
| "You're building confidence intervals around a stain." | **Jargon applied literally to something absurd.** This is the house specialty. |
| "Good. I was hoping for violence." | **Wrong emotional reaction.** A crisis arrives and the General is *pleased*. |
| "I assume the missing pixels are in a separate attachment." | **Polite sarcasm** that treats a ridiculous situation as a routine admin problem. |
| "The model rejected the premise." / "That's not a bug. That's self-respect." | **The model is a character** with dignity. |
| "Unadjusted ratios are anecdotes with decimals." | **An aphorism** that's quotable and true, and that you'd steal for a real meeting. |
| "We were at war with a JPEG of nothing." | **The General's frame, deflated.** His epic language pointed at something tiny. |
| "Looking like an idiot is blending in." | **Reversal.** The advice you expected, flipped. |

Lines that are weaker, and why:
| Line | Problem |
|---|---|
| "I require no context!" | The villain states his own flaw. That's exposition, not a joke. |
| "Tea." | Too minimal. It isn't a reaction to anything, so it reads as random. |
| "Cooked." | Fine once as a button. As a recurring bit, it's a catchphrase standing in for a joke. |

## 2. Joke engines per character
Each character should *produce* jokes by applying their lens to the situation, not by restating their personality.

### Nils: kindness that lands like violence
He's the most reasonable person in the room, and that is the joke. He helps too much, explains too well, and takes things literally.
- ✅ "I'm not upset. I've simply updated my prior on you."
- ✅ "Happy to help! First, let's define 'help'. I've prepared slides."
- ✅ (to the broker, sincerely) "Your data isn't wrong. It's just not *about* anything."
- ✅ "It's not an opinion. It has a p-value."
- ❌ "As a calm person, I will remain calm." (restates the trait)

### General Hrom: disproportionate force, correctly aimed
He's epic about tiny things and never wrong about the numbers. Twist war-film quotes onto office life rather than quoting them straight.
- ✅ "I love the smell of reserve strengthening in the morning."
- ✅ "Nobody leaves this room until the triangle confesses."
- ✅ "Send the broker my regards. In a crate."
- ✅ "In Slovakia we have a word for this spreadsheet. We do not say it in front of children."
- ❌ "I am very angry about the broker!" (volume isn't a joke)

### A²: Gen-Z as a precise technical language
The slang is **correct analysis in a different dialect**. Funniest when her translation is *more* accurate than the jargon.
- ✅ "So chain ladder is a séance with Excel."
- ✅ "'Straightforward risk' is a red flag wearing a blazer."
- ✅ "This loss ratio is not giving what it's supposed to give."
- ✅ "He said 'just a few large losses' like that's not the whole plot."
- ❌ "No cap fr fr slay." (slang with no content, which reads as cringe)
- Rule: **one slang term per line at most**, and the line has to be true.

### Sudo: deadpan competence
She says nothing for three panels, then fixes everything in one sentence and is mildly bored by it.
- ✅ "Fixed it. Also fixed the thing you were about to break."
- ✅ "The data's clean now. We had a conversation."
- ✅ "I automated your complaint. It runs every Monday."
- ✅ (on a 400-tab Excel file) "I'm not opening it. I'm having it assessed."
- ❌ "I am good at coding." (trait, no joke)

## 3. Episode structure (6 panels)
1. **Inciting absurdity.** A real, recognisable pain from our work, exaggerated one notch.
2. **The team reacts.** Each character through their lens. Get the first laugh here.
3. **Escalation.** Someone (usually the General) overreacts in the wrong direction.
4. **The actuarial turn.** The real technical point, delivered as a joke. This is what makes it ours.
5. **Reversal.** Sudo or Nils solves it, or the broker gets what's coming.
6. **The button.** A final twist or callback that recontextualises the episode. **Never end on a summary.**

## 4. Rules of the room
1. **The line has to survive without the picture.** Read the dialogue aloud. If it only works because of the art, the art may not come out right.
2. **Specific beats generic.** "IMG_4837_FINAL_v2_REAL.jpg" is funnier than "a bad file".
3. **Write 3 versions of every key line**, then pick one. The first version is usually the obvious one.
4. **Cut the setup, keep the punchline.** Balloons hold about 10 words. Most jokes get funnier when shorter.
5. **Rule of three**, with the third item being the wrong one.
6. **One catchphrase per episode at most**, and only if it lands a beat and doesn't replace one.
7. **Punchline word last.** "That's not a bug. That's *self-respect*." The funny word goes at the end of the balloon.
8. **The lunch test.** Would a colleague repeat the line at lunch? If not, punch it up or cut it.

## 5. Punch-up checklist (run before sending anything to ChatGPT)
- [ ] Does every panel have at least one surprise?
- [ ] Is any line just a character stating their trait? Rewrite it.
- [ ] Is the actuarial point correct?
- [ ] Is the final panel a button rather than a summary?
- [ ] Is every balloon 12 words or fewer?
- [ ] Is there at least one background gag (mug, duck, screen text)?
